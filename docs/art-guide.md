# Art guide

- **Style:** isometric pixel art in full color, nearest-neighbour filtering, no anti-aliasing. Shade in steps, never with smooth gradients; ordered dithering between steps is fine.
- **Colors:** each material has a ramp, dark to light, at the top of `art/gen_art.gd`. Ramps shift hue: shadows lean cool, highlights warm. Reuse a ramp, or add one for a new material.
- **Light:** comes from the top left. Characters get a 1 px dark outline and a translucent ground shadow.
- **Tiles:** 96×48 px diamonds (PixelLab's isometric size). `assets/tiles.png` comes ready-made from the PixelLab kit (not from `art/gen_art.gd`): copy the kit's `tiles.png` and set each ground's `full_variants` in `data/tiles/<id>.json`. One row per ground (the `atlas` field), 32 columns. Columns 1-14 are the ground cut out in alpha for each corner mask of the dual grid (`client/dual_grid.gd`: `NW << 3 | NE << 2 | SW << 1 | SE`, where NW is the top corner on screen, NE the right, SW the left and SE the bottom): only that ground is opaque, so it lays over any lower ground. Column 0 is empty, column 15 is the full tile. The lowest ground (deep water) is always drawn full and needs no masks. Full tiles cover the whole diamond (PixelLab draws one extra row on the edge; overlapping neighbours is fine). Tile size and atlas layout are set in one place, `client/iso_layer.gd`.
- **Full tile variants:** after the 16 mask columns, a row holds the other versions of its full tile (`full_variants` in `data/tiles/<id>.json`, up to `IsoLayer.MAX_FULL_VARIANTS` = 17; column `IsoLayer.atlas_column(15, v)`). Each drawn full tile picks one from a hash of its position. Variants must tile with each other and with the masks: same colours and brightness, no rim. `mask_variants` is reserved for versions of the transition tiles and must stay 1 for now.
- **Objects:** PNGs from the PixelLab kit, with their ground shadow drawn in. `data/objects/<id>.json` lists one or more `sprites`, each a `file` and an `anchor_px`: the pixel that stands on the cell centre (the foot of the trunk, the middle of a rock's base). Each cell picks one sprite from a hash. Wall sprites run along the grid's y axis (up-right on screen); the game mirrors them where a wall continues along x.
- **Ground tint:** the game multiplies ground layers by a slow world-space noise (±6 % brightness, a little warmer when lighter; `ground_tint` in `data/biomes.json`, `client/ground_tint.gdshader`). Draw grounds at their normal brightness; do not bake large-scale shading into tiles.
- **Decals:** small flat details over the ground (tufts, pebbles, flowers, leaves, twigs, mud, litter), PNGs from the PixelLab kit. `data/decals/<id>.json` lists its `sprites`; each placed decal picks one from a hash and is drawn centred on its position. They lie flat: players and objects never hide behind them. Keep them small (a fraction of a tile) and close to the ground's colours, so they break the pattern without drawing the eye.
- **Characters:** 64×96 px frames (`WalkAnim.FRAME_SIZE`), feet at the bottom, centered. Rows: standing, then a walk cycle of 4 frames over two steps (`WalkAnim.WALK_FRAMES`; poses in `POSES` in `art/gen_art.gd`). Players face 8 directions defined on screen, in this order (the names and order of PixelLab's 8 directions, `WalkAnim.FACINGS`): south, south-east, east, north-east, north, north-west, west, south-west. Today `assets/player.png` has 4 columns (facing down, left, right, up); `WalkAnim.FACING_COLUMN` picks the column drawn for each of the 8 facings. A full sheet has 8 columns in the order above, one row per frame (standing, then the walk cycle); to use it, set `WalkAnim.SHEET_COLUMNS` to 8 and `WalkAnim.FACING_COLUMN` to `[0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7]`.
- **Scale:** the camera shows art at 1 texel per screen pixel (zoom 1). Draw details at this size; do not upscale smaller art.
- **Format:** PNG, RGBA, no metadata you would not want public.
- **Generated art:** prefer extending `art/gen_art.gd` (regenerate with `make art`). Images from other tools are fine with a full `ASSETS.toml` entry.
