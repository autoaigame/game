# Art guide

- **Style:** isometric pixel art in full color, nearest-neighbour filtering, no anti-aliasing. Shade in steps, never with smooth gradients; ordered dithering between steps is fine.
- **Colors:** the muted survival palette of the PixelLab kit (40 colours): desaturated natural greens, browns and greys, soft light, no cartoon look.
- **Light:** comes from the top left. Objects and characters carry a translucent ground shadow.
- **Tiles:** 96×48 px diamonds (PixelLab's isometric size). `assets/tiles.png` is made from PixelLab tilesets, assembled and cut by script outside the repo (not by `art/gen_art.gd`): to change it, replace the PNG, keep its layout, and set each ground's `full_variants` in `data/tiles/<id>.json`. One row per ground (the `atlas` field), 32 columns. Columns 1-14 are the ground cut out in alpha for each corner mask of the dual grid (`client/dual_grid.gd`: `NW << 3 | NE << 2 | SW << 1 | SE`, where NW is the top corner on screen, NE the right, SW the left and SE the bottom): only that ground is opaque, so it lays over any lower ground. Column 0 is empty, column 15 is the full tile. The lowest ground (deep water) is always drawn full and needs no masks. Full tiles cover the whole diamond (PixelLab draws one extra row on the edge; overlapping neighbours is fine). Tile size and atlas layout are set in one place, `client/iso_layer.gd`.
- **Full tile variants:** after the 16 mask columns, a row holds the other versions of its full tile (`full_variants` in `data/tiles/<id>.json`, up to `IsoLayer.MAX_FULL_VARIANTS` = 17; column `IsoLayer.atlas_column(15, v)`). Each drawn full tile picks one from a hash of its position. Variants must tile with each other and with the masks: same colours and brightness, no rim. `mask_variants` is reserved for versions of the transition tiles and must stay 1 for now.
- **Objects:** PNGs from the PixelLab kit, with their ground shadow drawn in. The shadow sits mostly behind the base, peeks out to the bottom right and is darkest where the object meets the ground; a shadow hanging below the base makes the object float. `art/object_shadows.gd` draws it (`godot --headless --path . -s res://art/object_shadows.gd`; give a new sprite its radii there), and `tests/test_object_shadows.gd` checks it. `data/objects/<id>.json` lists one or more `sprites`, each a `file` and an `anchor_px`: the pixel that stands on the cell centre (the foot of the trunk, the middle of a rock's base). Each cell picks one sprite from a hash. Wall sprites run along the grid's y axis (up-right on screen); the game mirrors them where a wall continues along x.
- **Ground tint:** the game multiplies ground layers by a slow world-space noise (±6 % brightness, a little warmer when lighter; `ground_tint` in `data/biomes.json`, `client/ground_tint.gdshader`). Draw grounds at their normal brightness; do not bake large-scale shading into tiles.
- **Decals:** small flat details over the ground (tufts, pebbles, flowers, leaves, twigs, mud, litter), PNGs from the PixelLab kit. `data/decals/<id>.json` lists its `sprites`; each placed decal picks one from a hash and is drawn centred on its position. They lie flat: players and objects never hide behind them. Keep them small (a fraction of a tile) and close to the ground's colours, so they break the pattern without drawing the eye.
- **Characters:** players are 3D models drawn as pixel art (`client/character_3d.gd`): one texel per screen pixel, every pixel a colour of `data/palette.json`, light in three flat bands, a 1 px dark outline and a ground shadow like the objects'. The body and its animations are made by `art/character/body.py` (`art/character/BODY.md`); the top of the head is 100 px above the feet.
- **Character colours:** `data/palette.json` holds the kit's 40 colours and 12 for characters only, never used by the world's art. Skin tones from deep to fair: `2e221c`, `47322a`, `5f4434` (deep), `8a6149` and `d6b49b` (between and above the kit's three skin tones). Faded reds for hair and cloth: `4a2d24`, `6b3f30`, `8a5640`, `a46c52` (rust) and `5a2f2a`, `7a3f36`, `96564a` (brick). Each is muted like the kit (saturation under 0.6). How parts are made: `docs/character-parts.md`.
- **Dog:** `assets/dog.png` is drawn by script (`art/dog_sheet.gd`, run by `make art`): frames of 64×48 px (`DogAnim.FRAME_SIZE`), feet `DogAnim.FOOT_PX` px above the bottom, centred. One facing (east); the game mirrors it to face west. 4 columns of frames, one row per pose in `DogAnim.ROWS` order: idle, walk, run, sit, sleep, pee, dig. Change the script, then run `make art`; `tests/test_dog_art.gd` checks the PNG matches it.
- **Bag icons:** each item has a 32×32 icon, `assets/items/<id>.png`, drawn by `art/ui_icons.gd` with the slot frames (`assets/ui/slot*.png`, 9-slice, 5 px margins) and the fire and hunger icons. Muted colours, light from the top left, flat shading steps, a darker edge of the shape's own colour instead of a black outline. A new item needs its icon there (`tests/test_bag.gd` checks it); until then the bag shows a square of its `color`.
- **Scale:** the camera shows art at 1 texel per screen pixel (zoom 1). Draw details at this size; do not upscale smaller art.
- **Menu:** the start menu's art (`assets/ui/`: title backdrop, logo, button and panel frames, title band, focus frame) is made by `art/ui_menu.gd` (run by `make art`) from PixelLab images kept in `art/ui_menu/src/`: quantized to the kit plus three amber tones (`7a4a1e`, `b8762a`, `e0a845`, the colossus's eye, the lenses and the rust; only the menu uses them), frames rebuilt as 9-slices whose edges repeat one short period. The title backdrop (480×270) and the logo are the only art drawn at 2 screen pixels per texel (`UiTheme.BACKDROP_SCALE`, `LOGO_SCALE`); everything else in the menu stays at 1.
- **Format:** PNG, RGBA, without text or time chunks (strip them: they can carry tool settings, paths or dates).
- **Where the art comes from:** the world art (tiles, objects, decals) was made with PixelLab and retouched by script, the 3D characters from a CC0 mannequin reshaped by script; the menu art in `assets/ui/` mixes PixelLab images and pixels drawn by hand. Images from PixelLab or other tools are welcome with a full `ASSETS.toml` entry: the exact tool and settings, the prompt and seed, and every retouch. `make art` (`art/gen_art.gd`) draws only the dog sheet, the bag's icons and the menu art from their scripts; it calls no generator.
